\section{End-to-end protocol}\label{sec:e2e}

\subsection{Bytecode encoding}\label{sec:e2e-bc}

The program is public, so nothing is decoded in a table: both parties decode each of the $\nprog=2^{\kbc}$ instruction words once, into the fields the rows read (\S\ref{sec:bytecode}), and encode the result as one $\K$-valued multilinear $P$ in $\kbc+4$ variables:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item lay instruction $z$ out as $16$ slots of $\K$: the class tag $\opc{j}$ in slot $2$ (\S\ref{sec:bytecode}); the flags in slot $3$, a word of selector bits naming the class's function, the finalization word for \texttt{HASH}, the accumulate and base-field bits for \texttt{EXT}; the register numbers $a_1,a_2,a_d$ in slots $4$ to $6$, $\intk{\sink}=\intk{32}$ for an instruction with no destination or with $\regs[0]$ as one; the immediate in slot $7$, sign-extended, and folded with $\pc$ for \texttt{auipc}; the successor $\intk{\tbase+4z+4}$ in slot $8$; for the control class, $\Delta$ in slot $9$, the branch or jump target XOR the successor, and the bits $\mathit{link}$ and $\mathit{jalr}$ in slots $10$ and $11$ (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}); the exit selector $\exitflag$ in slot $13$, $1$ only for \texttt{ecall}; and $0$ everywhere else: slot $12$, where a division's row puts its verdict (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}), and slots $0$, $1$, $14$ and $15$, which the separator, the address and nothing occupy in the bus tuple. A slot of the text holding no instruction the machine defines, the halt slot included, is all zeros: its tag is no table's.
\item read the resulting $2^{\kbc}\times16$ table as $P$, the low $\kbc$ variables indexing the instruction and the high four its slot, bits low first.
\end{enumerate}
So $P(z,0,1,0,0)$ is instruction $z$'s tag, $P(z,0,0,1,0)$ its first register, $P(z,0,0,0,1)$ its successor, and $P(z,0,0,0,0)=P(z,1,1,1,1)=0$.

\paragraph{Well-formedness.} The proof system is sound for any table $P$, a malformed one included: it proves that the rows read $P$ and did what its fields say. What makes $P$ a RISC-V program is checked once, by both parties, where a program enters: every entry with a tag reads two registers below $32$ and writes a cell in $1..32$, its successor is its own address plus $4$, its flags are ones its class defines, its selector bits are bits, a \texttt{LOAD} or \texttt{LD} entry reads $\regs[0]$ as its second register, a \texttt{STORE} or \texttt{SD} entry writes the sink, an \texttt{LD} or \texttt{SD} entry has flags $0$, a \texttt{HASH} entry writes the sink and has immediate $0$, and an \texttt{EXT} entry reads its address from a register, not the sink, and has immediate $0$. The two verifiers check the same rules, and the digest below is of the decoded table, so a verifier is never handed a program it did not check. Both also cap the table at $\nprog\le2^{21}$ slots, the padding and the halt slot included, which the bus's soundness needs (\S\ref{sec:e2e-ledger}).

\subsection{The statement}\label{sec:e2e-pi}

The \emph{statement} is a program and an output: that the program, run on RAM holding its image, halts on \texttt{exit} with $\regs[10],\dots,\regs[13]$ holding the output (\S\ref{sec:vm}). The program is one digest, BLAKE2s of everything public and fixed: the table $P$, the entry point, the halt slot, $\kmem$, $\kadv$ and the image, each variable-length part length-framed. The digest seeds the Fiat-Shamir transcript (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}), so every challenge depends on the exact statement; the output does too, as the words the transcript starts from.

RAM's initial words need no claim: its seed column $\meminit$ is public, the image then zeros, and the verifier evaluates its extension at $\zeta$ itself, in time proportional to the image rather than to $\nmem$, the column being zero past the image (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}). The output and the exit are five claims on the committed final registers $\regfin$ at Boolean points, one per register, whose values both parties know and neither transmits:
\[
  \mle{\regfin}(\mathrm{bits}(17))\;\qeq\;\intk{93},\qquad \mle{\regfin}(\mathrm{bits}(10+i))\;\qeq\;\textsf{output}_i\quad(i<4),
\]
with $\mathrm{bits}(r)\in\cube{6}$ the number of the register, low bit first. The PCS opening binds them to the committed column, and Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem} makes that column what the run's last writes left, so the run executed \texttt{exit} with those values in place. The initial registers are the constant zero, the advice's initial words are the prover's, and nothing else about the run is public.

\subsection{Padding the tables}\label{sec:e2e-pad}

A table is proven over $2^{\tau_j}$ rows, its announced height $\nlive{j}$ padded to a power of two and to the batch floors (\S\ref{sec:tables}). Rows $\nlive{j}$ onwards are \emph{padding rows}, all equal to row $\nlive{j}$; an honest prover makes it a no-op of the table's class at clock $0$ on zero registers, an entry the text carries past the program and an illegal word, so that its circuit instances are honest ones. The commitment stores the padding row once (\S\ref{sec:jagged}).

The padding rows are off the bus: a table's blocks put the identity leaf $1$ on every row from $\nlive{j}$ on (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}), so the two products are over the rows the run made and nothing else, which is what makes a height binding: moving it moves rows on or off the bus. The table sumcheck still sums each form over all $2^{\tau_j}$ rows, so the prover sends the padding row's column values $\padrow{j}$, the verifier computes each block's leaf $\ell_b$ on them, and adds $\eq(\mathsf{sel}_b,\zeta_{\ge\tau_j})\,\tailw{\nlive{j}}(\zeta_{<\tau_j})\,(\ell_b-1)$ back to the side's share, exactly what the identity leaves took away. The values $\padrow{j}$ join the claim pool, one claim per column at the Boolean point naming row $\nlive{j}$, so they are the committed ones. This is the jagged PCS's padded-row adjustment~\cite{jagged_pcs} with the padding row the prover's, committed and opened, rather than a zero row.

A row of the run with a clock lacking the live bit is still inert, as before: its state tuples cancel because its clock does not advance, and its accesses meet nothing of the run because the order check \eqref{eq:order} keeps the live bit off its previous timestamps (Theorem~\ref{thm:rwmem}).

\subsection{Fiat Shamir instantiation}\label{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}

The transcript is a BLAKE2s sponge over $\K$-words. Its initial state is the program's digest (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}), and the four output words are absorbed first, so the state depends on the whole statement before the prover sends anything. Every scalar the prover sends is absorbed as it is sent, every challenge is squeezed from the state at that point, and a value that rode the stream is never absorbed a second time. The commitment's Merkle root is a scalar like any other.

\subsection{The unrolled protocol}\label{sec:e2e-unrolled}

Each reduction below adds evaluation claims on columns to a shared \emph{claim pool}, proved by the final PCS \textsc{Opening} phase.

\paragraph{Setup.}

The Fiat-Shamir state is seeded by the statement (\S\ref{sec:e2e-fiat-shamir}). The prover sends the eleven table heights $\nlive{j}$, the PCS rate and the final clock $\tsfinal\in\K$ (\S\ref{sec:state}); the verifier checks each $\nlive{j}$ against the cap $2^{32}$, derives $\tau_j$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}), checks that $\tsfinal$ is a live clock at slot $0$, and derives the layout of every stack (PCS pieces and GKR leaves) and the producer's bit count $\nbit$ (\S\ref{sec:lookup}) from those sizes and the program's.

\paragraph{Commitment.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover.} Stack (\S\ref{sec:stacking}) every column: the read-write arrays' final columns and timestamps, $\regfin,\memfin,\advfin$ and their $\tsfin$, the advice's initial words $\advinit$ (\S\ref{sec:memchan}), the bytecode's multiplicity column $\mlt$ (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), the twenty-one packed witnesses, $\qcls{j}$ for the ten tables with a class circuit and $\qclk{j}$ for all eleven (\S\ref{sec:tab-class}, \S\ref{sec:clock}), and every committed column of the eleven tables, the last two cut into pieces (\S\ref{sec:jagged}), into $q$, and send its commitment (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}). Then send, for each table with padding rows, $\padrow{j}$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}).
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Receives the commitment to $q$ and the $\padrow{j}$, which join the claim pool after the table sumcheck's claims.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Bus.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Samples and sends the fingerprint challenge $\alpha\in\E^{4}$ and the multiset challenge $\beta\in\E$ (\S\ref{sec:gp}).
\item \textbf{Prover.} Forms the push and pull leaf vectors (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}), the push side ending on the bytecode producer's bit blocks (\S\ref{sec:lookup}), a table's rows from its height on holding $1$ (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pad}), stacked into blocks and padded with $1$s. Sends one bus root $R$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run the one batched GKR (\S\ref{sec:gkr}) over the two trees, at the depth $\mu$ of the taller. The verifier checks each side's product against $R$; one $R$ for both sides is the balance check (\S\ref{sec:gp}). The pass yields two leaf claims $\widetilde V^{s}_0(\zeta)$ at one shared $\zeta$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Decompose each leaf claim (\S\ref{sec:leafstack}). The verifier forms every public part itself: the block selectors, the constant entries, the index columns (\S\ref{sec:idxcol}) and RAM's seed column (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}). Four blocks per side belong to no table: the state boundary, plus the registers', RAM's and the advice's seed (push side) or finalization (pull side). For their committed columns the prover sends one evaluation each, entering the claim pool: on the push side the advice's initial column alone, on the pull side every final column and timestamp. Every other block belongs to a table or to a producer, and is discharged by the table sumcheck (\S\ref{sec:air}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Table sumcheck.} One run proves the eleven tables' share of the bus, and the producer's (\S\ref{sec:air}), and \texttt{EXT}'s three identities, the only constraints a table has (\S\ref{sec:tab-classes}).
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Samples and sends the batch challenge $\xi\in\E$. Its first $\nside=2$ powers weight the bus forms, one per side and shared by every table and the producer, and the next three, $\xi^{2}$ to $\xi^{4}$, \texttt{EXT}'s identities, whose sums are zero, so the target is the bus's alone. It draws no point: the batch runs at the bus point, $T_j$ tested at $\zeta_{<\tau_j}$ and a producer of $2^{\kappa}$ entries at $\zeta_{<\kappa}$.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run the sumcheck over the eleven tables and then the bytecode's producer, $\taumax$ rounds, $\taumax$ the largest of their log heights, binding $X_0$ first, so every table takes part from the first round and is done after as many rounds as its own log height (\S\ref{sec:air}), against the target the verifier derived from the two leaf claims. Its summand has individual degree three and the round check pins one of the four coefficients (\S\ref{sec:prelim}), so a round costs three $\E$-elements.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} The prover sends one value per column of every table, and each producer's bit values $\widetilde{\mbit{k}}(\fc_{<\kappa})$, $k<\nbit$; the verifier computes each producer's public columns $\widetilde{\pubp{k}}(\fc_{<\kappa})$ itself (\S\ref{sec:lookup}). It rebuilds each $B^{s}_j$ and \texttt{EXT}'s identities from them and checks that they reproduce the sumcheck's final value; the table column values then enter the claim pool, $T_j$'s at $\fc_{<\tau_j}$. A circuit's ports are columns of its table but live in $\qcls{j}$ or $\qclk{j}$, so their claims route there (\S\ref{sec:tab-class}); a producer's bits are slices of its multiplicity column, so their claims go to ring switching (below).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The exit.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Pool the five claims on $\regfin$ at the Boolean points naming $\regs[17]$ and $\regs[10],\dots,\regs[13]$, with the values $\intk{93}$ and the output; the prover sends nothing (\S\ref{sec:e2e-pi}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Flock validity.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Run Flock's reduction (Annex~\ref{annex:flock}) once over the twenty-one circuits, batched under shared challenges (\S\ref{flock:batch}): every class circuit in table order, over $\qcls{j}$, \texttt{EXT} having none, then every clock circuit in table order, over $\qclk{j}$, each with $2^{\tau_j}$ instances. One zerocheck, its round-one message shared by all twenty-one, and one lincheck leave one evaluation claim on each packed witness, and one claim on each circuit's matrix form, which the verifier settles by one walk of the circuit once the proof is read.
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Sends $\rsgamma\in\E$, drawn after every ring-switched claim is bound. The twenty-two claims are the twenty-one witnesses', then the bytecode's multiplicity column's, its suffix point being its $\fc_{<\kappa}$ and its $64$ slices the $\nbit$ bit values it sent, then zeros, which are not transmitted (\S\ref{sec:lookup}); claim $j$, from zero, takes the scale $\rsgamma^{\,j}$, and both sides form the family's slices $\sfam{i}=\sum_j\rsgamma^{\,j}s_{j,i}$ (\S\ref{rs:family}). The prover sends nothing.
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} The ring switching of \S\ref{sec:ringswitch}, its map drawn after $\rsgamma$, reduces the family to one weighted-sum claim on the stack, target $\sum_ix^i\Phi(\sfam{i})$, with an MLE-friendly $\E$-valued weight (Definition~\ref{def:ipcs}) supported on the pieces of the twenty-two columns, claim $j$'s on a piece being $\Phi\bigl(\rsgamma^{\,j}\pscale\,\eq(r_j,\cdot)\bigr)$, $\pscale$ its scale on the piece (\S\ref{sec:jagged}); it joins the pool, ahead of every column claim. The circuits of one block size leave points that are prefixes of one another, so the verifier evaluates their weights in one pass (\S\ref{rs:weight}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Opening.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Each pooled claim is one evaluation of a column at a point, its value already supplied; via the stacking selectors (\S\ref{sec:stacking}) and the column's pieces (\S\ref{sec:jagged}) it becomes a weighted sum $\sum_w W_j(w)\,\widetilde q(w)=c_j$ over the stacked witness, one scaled equality term per piece. (The ring-switched claim arrives already in this form, its weight supported on the pieces of the twenty-two columns.)
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Sends one batching challenge $\lambda\in\E$, drawn after every claim value is bound. Claim $j$ takes the weight $\lambda^{j-1}$, the ring-switched claim first, with $\lambda^0=1$, and the pooled column claims after: the $J$ weights are distinct powers of the one challenge (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}).
\item \textbf{Prover \& Verifier.} Fold the $J$ claims into $W_\lambda=\sum_j\lambda^{j-1} W_j$ and $C_\lambda=\sum_j\lambda^{j-1} c_j$, and run the inner-product PCS opening on $\sum_w W_\lambda(w)\,\widetilde q(w)=C_\lambda$ (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}), the verifier evaluating $W_\lambda$ itself. This one opening discharges every pooled claim; there is no separate reduction sumcheck.
\item \textbf{Verifier.} Accepts if every check above passed: the caps, the one bus root for both sides, every sumcheck's rounds and final value, flock's batched reduction, and the PCS opening.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Soundness ledger}\label{sec:e2e-ledger}

The commitment takes no out-of-domain sample (Annex~\ref{annex:pcs}), so its root binds the prover to the list $\polyset_{\mathsf{cm}}$ of at most $L_0=\tfrac{1}{2\slack_0\sqrt{\rate_0}}$ polynomials (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}), not to one of them. Every challenge drawn after the root and before the \textsc{Opening} is a test the prover may pass with any member of that list: the bus's $\alpha$ and $\beta$ and its GKR's challenges, the table sumcheck's $\xi$ and rounds, Flock's, $\rsgamma$ and the ring-switching map. The opening holds the prover only to some member satisfying every pooled claim, so a test that a fixed polynomial passes with probability $\varepsilon$ is passed by some member with probability at most $L_0\,\varepsilon$, by a union bound over the list. Each such challenge's error is therefore $L_0$ times the one its own section states. Over every size and rate the PCS configures, $\log_2L_0\le12$, a bound the implementation pins to the derivation of the PCS parameters; so a challenge before the opening keeps $128$ bits when its own error is at most $2^{-140}$.

The round-by-round errors, in bits ($-\log_2$ of the error), at their smallest over every configured size and rate:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \emph{Bus} (\S\ref{sec:gp}, \S\ref{sec:gkr}): $4N/|\E|$ for the fingerprint, $N$ the larger side's linear factors, plus at most $8(\mu+1)^2/|\E|$ for the GKR; times $L_0$, at least $128$. The bytecode producer dominates $N$ with up to $2^{\kbc}(2^{\nbit}-1)$ factors (\S\ref{sec:lookup}). In a layout one commitment holds, the multiplicity column and every table's packed witness share the $2^{28}$ committed words, so $\nbit\le28$ and every block has at most $2^{28}$ rows; with $\kbc\le21$ the whole degree is below $2^{52}$, which leaves $140$ bits before the union and $128$ after. The native verifier also refuses an announced layout whose bus would miss this margin.
\item \emph{Table sumcheck} (\S\ref{sec:air}): $(3\taumax+\nside+\nconstot)/|\E|$ with $\taumax\le32$; at least $172$ after the union.
\item \emph{Flock} (Annex~\ref{flock:soundness}): below $2^{-183}$; at least $171$ after the union.
\item \emph{Ring switching} (Lemma~\ref{lem:rs-family}): $(\nrs-1)/|\E|+2^{-160}$; at least $147$ after the union.
\item \emph{Opening} (Theorem~\ref{thm:rbr}): each row already carries its level's list size $L_i$, so no further union applies. At the configured parameters the fold challenges' MCA errors clear $128$ bits, the batching challenges $168$, the OOD challenges $150$, and each query phase $111$ bits from its queries and $17$ from grinding.
\end{itemize}
Every row clears $128$ bits, so the Fiat-Shamir compilation of the whole protocol keeps the $128$-bit target (\S\ref{sec:iopcs}).
